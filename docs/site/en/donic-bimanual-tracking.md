# Can a DONIC tracking score tell whether a handoff succeeded?

Imagine the left hand supporting a cup while the right hand takes it and moves it away. If the left hand lets go too soon, the cup briefly loses support. The right hand can recover it, and both hands can still have small average pose errors over the full clip. DONIC shows how a single controller can learn different roles for the two hands across references. It gives DexLab a narrower question to ask. **When a clip passes the tracking thresholds, what evidence shows that the handoff also succeeded?**

## How both hands follow one reference

The controller gets the next reference for both hands and the cup. It corrects the hand targets using the current state and receives feedback on the cup’s position, rotation, and velocity. When the reference first calls for the left hand to support the cup, the left hand receives contact supervision. During the transfer each hand gets its own feedback. Once the left hand is meant to release, it leaves the contact reward. The object-motion term stays active throughout. The reward averages the participating hands, so one hand can still receive useful feedback when the other loses contact. [Paper §4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29997v1#S4.SS3)

Training makes two further changes. It subtracts the mean PPO advantage within each reference group. For example, −5 and −3 become −1 and 1, keeping their difference of 2. It also collects actions from successful specialist or shared-policy runs and replays them to check object displacement and required lifting. Accepted actions train the policy in a separate behavior-cloning batch. DexTrack introduced this demonstration loop, which DONIC combines with per-hand contact supervision and clip-centered advantages. [DONIC §4.4 and Appendix C.3–C.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29997v1#A3) · [DexTrack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09614)

## What the success rate measures

The authors evaluated single-rigid-object tracking in Isaac Gym. With 1,845 public training clips, one frozen DONIC policy passed **46/53 (86.79%)** new trajectories of training objects and **94/164 (57.32%)** trajectories of held-out objects. The stronger adapted baseline, Dexplore-bi, passed **5/53** and **19/164**. Adding 1,575 BiHOI references raised the counts to **47/53** and **98/164**. This addition changed both the size and distribution of the data, so the results cannot isolate the benefit of more simultaneous contact. The counts are reconstructed from the published percentages and split sizes. The project page notes that DexTrack-bi uses its own reference conversion and scene. [Paper Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29997v1#S5.T2) · [Project comparisons](https://ropedia.github.io/DONIC/#compare)

Success rate (SR) applies thresholds to clip-mean errors and requires both hands to pass with the object. Completion (Comp) applies those thresholds frame by frame and counts valid frames after a recovery. On held-out objects, removing clip centering drops SR to 48.17%. Removing the 3 cm proximity condition from the net-force contact proxy drops it to 48.78%, with the object reward and per-hand aggregation retained. These splits measure reference-conditioned tracking in simulation. Autonomous tasks, unseen operation categories and hardware performance each require their own evaluation. [Paper Appendix D.2–D.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29997v1#A4)

## What to check during the handoff

DONIC estimates object contact when fingertip net force exceeds 0.5 N and the fingertip is within 3 cm of the object. A fingertip pressing on the table beside the cup can satisfy both conditions. Collision pairs can identify whether the fingertip touched the cup. Force direction, impulse and the cup’s subsequent motion can help determine whether it was supported. [Paper §4.3 and Appendix C.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29997v1#A3.SS2)

DexLab can start with a fixed support, transfer and release reference and one valid control. In the first designed counterexample, a fingertip near the cup touches only the table. The scorer should identify the wrong contact partner. In the second, the left hand releases early and the cup drops before the right hand provides support. The scorer should be able to mark the handoff as failed even if the clip still passes tracking. Report tracking error, collision pairs and the phase outcome on the same timeline. Missing observations should leave the affected judgment indeterminate. An offline pilot can inspect what existing records contain and then use labeled synthetic cases to check the scoring rules. [DexLab Discussion #423](https://github.com/huangkiki/Dexlab/discussions/423) · [Scoring semantics Discussion #302](https://github.com/huangkiki/Dexlab/discussions/302)

## Available code and data

BiHOI covers 1,575 clips, 229 objects and 14.77 hours of unique interaction. Among frames with at least one hand in contact, 65.91% have both hands in contact. The pinned public pipeline processes multi-view video into object poses, meshes and MANO parameters for both hands. The full hand pipeline requires additional assets, and the original processing code uses CC BY-NC 4.0. The policy training and evaluation code, checkpoints and BiHOI dataset are listed as pending. Hugging Face hosts the paper entry. The experimental numbers above are author-reported.
